Why real data

The earlier pieces in this series simulated their data and graded the analytics against a sealed answer key: that proves the method, not the world. Here nothing is planted, so every number has to survive the source.

Every reading gets one disposition

Each reading of the six AQI pollutants receives exactly one disposition, valid or a named issue, and only valid readings reach the facts. {{dq_kinds}} kinds of issue are logged across readings, hours, days and stations, each with its action and count (the report's trust page, below). {{set_aside_kinds}} of them set readings aside, {{set_aside}} in all. The rest keep the reading and count it: {{ceiling_n}} particulate readings sit at the analyser's ceiling of 1000 µg/m³, where the true value may be higher, and {{incomplete_n}} station-days have fewer than {{day_hours}} valid hours and get no daily value or AQI.

The pipeline

Discovery and download are separate, resumable scripts; the download refuses a plan above its budget and writes a manifest with every file's URL, size, MD5 and SHA-256. Staging types every reading, converts timestamps to IST (readings are period-ending: a 17:00 stamp belongs to the 16:00 hour), applies the unit rules and gives each reading its one disposition. The model layer builds the dimensions and hourly and daily facts; a day needs {{day_hours}} valid hours. The marts score CPCB's National AQI (24-hour means for particulates, NO2 and SO2, the highest 8-hour mean for O3 and CO; three pollutants including PM2.5 or PM10 for a station AQI; the city AQI as the mean of its stations), then months, seasons, exceedance of the national standards, spikes, hourly profiles and completeness.

The export to Excel and Power BI is blocked if any check fails. The workbook ({{xl_sheets}} sheets, {{xl_tables}} Excel Tables, month by city matrices coloured by AQI category) and the Power BI project are both generated from the same warehouse. A full build from the raw files took about {{build_minutes}} minutes.

Proving it

  • {{checks}} reconciliation checks pass on every build: every raw row staged once, one disposition per reading, hourly and daily values recomputed from their readings, no hour or day invented, AQI sub-indices against an independent implementation of CPCB's breakpoints, city and month figures recounted, spike episodes rebuilt, data-quality counts reconciled and the unit evidence retested.
  • Mutation test, {{mutations}} caught. A copy of the warehouse is broken once per check (a duplicated reading, an invented hour, a wrong sub-index, a spike run cut short...). Each break must fail its check, or the check is not proving anything.
  • {{report_match}} report numbers match. The report is opened in its own Power BI window; {{measures_ok}} measures evaluate without error, and every figure on the {{pbi_pages}} pages (cards, answer sentences rebuilt word for word, heatmap cells, the spike table, the issue log) is compared with independent SQL.
  • Nothing clipped. {{fit_lines}} single lines and {{fit_wrapped}} wrapped texts are measured with the report's real fonts against their boxes, under every slicer combination; {{fit_grids}} grids fit without scrollbars.
  • Byte-identical rebuilds. Two builds from the raw files give the same {{identical}} output files (every exported table and the workbook); a regenerated Power BI project matches the committed files.

The report

{{pbi_pages_word}} pages, generated as a Power BI project (a TMDL model of {{pbi_tables}} tables, {{pbi_rels}} relationships and {{pbi_measures}} measures, and a PBIR report) by powerbi/build_pbip.py. Short spikes, found from the data alone

There is no event calendar, no labels and no cause: a spike is defined only by how a day compares with the days around it.

For each city-day, the reference is the median city PM2.5 of the days {{sp_ref_from}} to {{sp_window}} days before and after it. The {{sp_guard}} days either side are left out, so an episode of up to {{sp_max}} days never sets its own reference, and a day is evaluated only when {{sp_side}} of the {{sp_ref_days}} reference days are measured on each side. A spike day is at least {{sp_ratio}}x its reference: {{sp_days}} of {{sp_eval}} evaluated city-days ({{sp_days_share}}). A short spike is a run of 1 to {{sp_max}} such days with measured ordinary days on both sides.

{{sp_n}} episodes: {{sp_by_city}}. Per 100 evaluated city-days they start most often in {{sp_top_month}} ({{sp_top_rate}}; {{sp_top_n}} of {{sp_top_days}} days) and least often in {{sp_low_month}} ({{sp_low_rate}}). A city value is a mean of stations, so each episode records how many stations carried it: in {{sp_single}} episodes a single station of several carries the peak day, and {{sp_alone}} rest on a day with only one station reporting. They are shown as such, not hidden. Not counted: {{sp_long}} runs longer than {{sp_max}} days and {{sp_edge}} runs next to a day without a reading or reference, where the length cannot be known.

Limits and next steps

  • The {{gap_months}}-month gap splits the record in two. Nothing can be said about how the air changed between {{gap_last}} and {{gap_first}}.
  • Station sets grow, so city averages across years are not like-for-like. A fixed panel of stations present in every year would be the honest way to compare years.
  • There is no weather or emissions data, so the analysis describes levels and timing, never causes. Joining IMD weather would be the next step towards explaining them.
  • CPCB's open-ended Severe band is closed at its lower bound plus the width of the band below; NH3 and lead are not in the archive. Power BI has no free public link, so the report ships as a project with these images.
